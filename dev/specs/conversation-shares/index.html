    Conversation shares: guest links to a session¶

    Status: implemented (2026-11-04; client protocol 1.88.0). The user's decisions are in section 13; how it landed is in section 15. A mockup of the guest page, the owner's view, and the share dialog is conversation-shares/preview.html. Builds on share links (the share store, /s/, the share-only port, expiry, the Sharing view) and the web client.

    1. Problem¶

    Share links publish one asset. The user also wants to bring someone into a conversation: let a colleague read what the agent did and carry on with it, without giving them the daemon token, which opens every session, setting, and machine.

    The user presses Share on a session and gets a URL. Whoever opens it gets that session in the web client as it is, live: the owner's own transcript, composer, and session panel. They can do what the owner does in the session, but not change how it is set up. Nothing else of the daemon.

    2. Goals and non-goals¶

    Goals

    • A conversation share is a public, unguessable URL for one session, in the same share store and /s/<id> space as asset links, with the same expiry, listing, and revoking.
    • A guest who opens it sees everything the transcript shows, live, as the owner does: messages, tool calls and results, thinking, the system prompt and tools rows, figures, attachments, and the delegated sessions under it.
    • A guest uses the session as the owner does: sends messages (with attachments), steers and queues, stops a run, answers approvals and elicitations, compacts, sets goals, runs skills, stops background calls, and reverts to an earlier message. Guests do not fork (decided 2026-11-04).
    • A guest sees the session panel whole, read-only where it changes the setup (section 6).
    • A guest cannot change the session's setup: permissions, runtime and machine bindings, tool exposure, model, agent, ACP agent options, or project. Nor can they reach any other session or daemon state.
    • The owner sees which messages a guest sent, and who is connected.
    • Revoking a link closes its guests' connections at once.

    Non-goals (this round)

    • Guest accounts, sign-in, or per-guest identities beyond the link's label (section 7).
    • Guests renaming or deleting sessions.
    • A read-only static export.
    • Spending limits per link (decided 2026-11-04: none).

    3. Conversation shares¶

    A record in shares.json beside the asset links; the records gain a target of asset or session.

    Field Meaning
    id 128 random bits, base64url, as for asset links. The URL is the capability.
    session The shared root session. Its delegated sessions come with it, read-only.
    label Who the link is for, such as "Alice"; "Guest" unless the owner names one. Messages sent through the link carry it.
    can_send Whether guests may act (send, answer, stop, compact, goal, skills, revert). Off makes a watch-only link. On by default.
    created, expires_at, views, last_viewed As for asset links: 7 days unless the owner picks 1 day, 30 days, or Never. views counts guest connections. 
    <share base>/s/<id>          the guest page
<share base>/s/<id>/ws       the guest connection
<share base>/s/_app/<file>   the web bundle

    Same base, warnings, and share-only port as asset links (share links section 6). The share-only port now also serves /s/<id>/ws and /s/_app/, and still nothing outside /s/.

    Lifecycle. Deleting the session's tree or its project removes the link (deleted_shares). Moving it to another project, compacting, reverting, or forking the session leaves the link on the same session; a fork the owner makes is not shared. Expiry or revocation ends the link: the page answers the uniform 404 and open guest connections close with "This link isn't available".

    4. What a guest can do¶

    The guest connection allows a fixed list of client protocol methods, each scoped to the shared tree. Every other method is refused with not_allowed_for_guest, new methods included, until someone adds them.

    Allowed
    Read (the shared session and its delegated sessions) Everything the session view and its panel read: events and history, cut results whole, the assets and artifacts the tree's events show, the session's meta, usage, permission rules and mode, attached runtimes, tool groups and skills, goal, background calls, and pending approvals
    Act (the shared session only, when can_send) Answering approvals and elicitations, session.revert at every cut point, session.submit_input with text and attachments (uploads under the daemon's usual limits), steering and queueing, recalling and dropping queued inputs, stopping the current run and background calls, session.compact and cancelling it, session.goal.set and the goal's lifecycle, reading and running skills (skills.get, sent as input), and the session's compact and goal commands for an ACP agent
    Refused session.select_model, session.set_permission_mode and rule changes, session.tools.set, session.runtime.attach/detach, session.agent_option.set, session.fork and Edit in fork, session.project.set, ACP agent commands other than compact and goal, rename, delete, pin and unread, and everything outside the session: other sessions, config, providers, runtimes, memory, projects, cron, assets lists, shares, plugins

    A turn a guest starts runs exactly as one the owner starts: the session's agent, model, tools, machines, and permission rules, with no budget of its own. Approvals go to every client on the session, guests included, and the first answer wins, as between the owner's own clients (see section 10).

    5. The guest connection¶

    /s/<id>/ws speaks the client protocol, so the web client talks to it as it talks to /ws, with three differences:

    • The link is the credential. The connection takes no token or page ticket, and is accepted from any Origin, null included (section 6). The daemon looks the link up when the socket opens and again at every request, so a revoked or expired link stops working mid-connection.
    • Requests are checked before dispatch against the list in section 4 and the shared tree. A request about any other session answers as if it did not exist.
    • Notifications are filtered the same way, fail closed: the guest gets the events and changes of its tree, and none of the daemon's broadcasts (other sessions in sessions.changed, config, runtimes, shares, usage across sessions).

    The hello a guest gets carries a guest marker, the shared session's ID, can_send, and the link's expiry, and none of the daemon's configuration, providers, runtimes, or agents. The web client reads the marker to enter guest mode.

    6. The guest page¶

    The web client's own bundle, in guest mode: the owner's session view as it is, with no guest-only views (decided 2026-11-04).

    • The same as the owner's: the transcript with everything it shows, the composer with attachments, steer and queue, and Stop, the session panel with every tab, the commands sheet, and the sheets the transcript opens: approvals, a message's actions (copy and revert; fork and Edit in fork are left out), and the agent's questions.
    • Usable: compact (the sheet and /compact), the goal tab and /goal, running a skill from the skills tab, stopping a background call, and everything in the transcript and composer.
    • Read-only: whatever changes the setup shows its current value and is disabled, with "Only the owner can change this": the permission mode and rules, attaching and detaching runtimes, the tool group and skill switches, the bottom bar's agent, model, and mode pickers, ACP agent options, and the project.
    • Not there: the sidebar and every other view, rename and delete.
    • A top bar with the cookie mark, the title, "shared with you as Alice", the expiry, and who is connected.

    How the code is reused. The web client already gates a read-only session with one check, Workspace::writable(), at 18 places. Guest mode splits it in two: writable() stays for acting (sending, compacting, the goal, running skills, attachments), and a new configurable() gates the setup (permission mode and rules, ACP options, which use writable() today) and the setup controls that have no gate yet (runtime attach, the tool group and skill switches, the model and agent pickers, and fork and Edit in fork in a message's menu). A guest is writable and not configurable; a watch-only guest is neither. The daemon's method list (section 4) refuses the same things whatever the page does. - Watch-only links (can_send off) show no composer. - Link previews: the page carries og:title (the session's title) and "A conversation shared from cookie agent".

    Its sandbox. On the main listener the page shares the daemon's origin, where the owner's browser keeps the token. So /s/<id> carries a CSP sandbox allow-scripts allow-popups header: the page runs in an opaque origin and cannot read the token, the page ticket cookie, or localStorage, nor open /ws as the daemon's page. Its socket to /s/<id>/ws comes from origin null, which that path accepts, and /s/_app/ answers with Access-Control-Allow-Origin: * so the bundle loads. Guest mode keeps no state in storage, which an opaque origin does not have. Whether the bundle boots under the sandbox is phase 1's first check; if it cannot, guest pages are served only on the share-only port.

    7. Attribution and presence¶

    • Messages sent through a link carry the label in their event's origin (section 15). The owner's clients show them with the label ("Alice · via link"), and so do guests'. The model gets the message exactly as if the owner had sent it: the label is for people, not for the agent (decided 2026-11-04).
    • Presence: the owner's session view shows the guests connected now, by label, beside its other clients, and the Sharing view shows each link's connected count.
    • Several guests on one link all appear under its label. To tell people apart, the owner makes one link each.

    8. Making and revoking¶

    Web client.

    • Share in the session header's menu, and in the ⋯ and right-click menus of its row in the session list, opens the share panel: the private-base warning when it applies, the warning of section 10 in one line, and a card for every link of the conversation, newest first (the label, who is connected, Can send, the URL with Copy, the expiry and views, Change expiry…, and Revoke, which asks first). New link opens a form in the panel (the label, Can send messages, the expiry, and Create link), and a conversation with no link opens on that form. With two or more links, Revoke all ends this conversation's links, and no others, and asks first (decided 2026-11-04).
    • A session with a live link shows the link badge in its header and sidebar row.
    • The Sharing view lists conversation shares beside asset links: the session, label, can_send, expiry, connected guests, and last view.

    CLI.

    cookie share session <session id> [--label <name>] [--watch-only]
                     [--expires 1d|7d|30d|never]

    cookie share list and cookie share revoke cover both kinds.

    TUI (decided 2026-11-04): /share makes a link with the defaults (label "Guest", can send, 7 days) and prints its URL, and /share <label> names it. Guests' messages show their label ("Alice · via link"), and connected guests show beside the session's other clients.

    Agents: no tool to share a session.

    9. Protocol and compatibility¶

    Client protocol, minor:

    • SessionShare { id, path, url?, session_id, title?, label, can_send, created, expires_at?, views, last_viewed?, connected };
    • SharesListResult gains sessions: Vec<SessionShare> (empty when absent) and SharesListParams gains session?, so older clients list asset links as before;
    • session_share.create { session_id, label?, can_send, expiry } and session_share.update { id, label?, can_send?, expiry? };
    • share.revoke, shares.revoke_all, and shares.changed cover both kinds, in one ID space;
    • the hello's guest marker (section 5), and the error not_allowed_for_guest;
    • session.delete and project.delete count both kinds in deleted_shares.

    History: no change: a guest's label is in the origin events already carry.

    Configuration: none new. [share] enabled turns both kinds off.

    Plugin and runtime protocols: no change.

    10. Security notes¶

    • A guest is the owner, inside one session. A guest's turn has the session's tools, machines, and permission rules, and the guest answers its approvals. So whatever the session's agent can do on any attached machine, a guest can: read ~/.ssh or cookie's config.toml with its provider keys, run commands, write files. A guest cannot widen the rules, change the setup, or reach another session. The dialog says this in one line, and the docs in full. Hand a link out as you would a shell on those machines, and make it watch-only otherwise.
    • A guest spends the owner's provider budget, without a limit (decided 2026-11-04). Expiry and revoking are the controls.
    • A link is a bearer secret that chat apps fetch to preview. It opens a connection only when the bundle runs, so an unfurler counts a view but sends nothing.
    • The method and notification lists fail closed: a method or broadcast added later is not reachable by guests until it is listed.
    • The control plane stays private behind the share-only port: it serves the guest socket, whose dispatcher refuses everything outside the list, and no other path.
    • The guest page cannot act as the daemon's page (section 6).

    11. Tests¶

    Expiry values in tests are explicit, never the default.

    • The method list: every allowed method works on the shared session; each refused one answers not_allowed_for_guest, including a method added to the protocol after the list (a test enumerates the method registry so a new method must be classified).
    • Scope: a request naming another session, or acting on a delegated one, answers as not found. Notifications of other sessions never arrive.
    • The hello holds no config, provider, runtime, or agent data.
    • A guest's revert works; a guest's fork is refused.
    • Watch-only links refuse every act.
    • Revoking or expiring a link closes its open connections and makes the next request fail.
    • A guest's origin carries its label, and the owner's view shows it.
    • A guest answers an approval and an elicitation; on a watch-only link the answer is refused.
    • Browser suite: the guest page boots under the sandbox, sends a message, answers an approval, compacts, sets a goal, runs a skill, sees the permissions and runtimes tabs with their controls disabled, and cannot read the daemon origin's localStorage or document.cookie.

    12. Phases¶

    1. Spike: the bundle in guest mode under the CSP sandbox, with a socket from origin null (section 6).
    2. Daemon: the record's target, the guest socket with its method and notification lists, the guest hello, via_share, presence, the protocol methods, and lifecycle.
    3. Web client: guest mode, the share dialog, badges, presence, and the Sharing view's rows.
    4. CLI and TUI.
    5. Docs: "Sharing" gains conversations with the warning of section 10; Remote access notes that the share port also serves guests.

    13. Decisions (2026-11-04)¶

    The user decided:

    1. Guests get the session as it is, not a filtered snapshot: they see everything the session shows and act in it. They cannot change permissions, runtime bindings, tool exposure, models, or agents.
    2. Everything else is the same as for the owner: guests answer approvals and elicitations, and their messages reach the model as the owner's do. No stricter mode for guests.
    3. No provider budget per link.
    4. Guests can revert too, but not fork (nor Edit in fork), to keep a link to one session. Rename and delete stay the owner's.
    5. Messages a guest sends show its link's label in the clients, not to the model.
    6. The TUI gets /share and shows guests' labels.
    7. Guests see the session panel with every tab, read-only where it changes the setup, and can compact, set goals, and run skills (revised later on 2026-11-04, after a version without the panel). The owner's views are reused as they are, split by writable() and configurable().
    8. Every link of a conversation shows in one panel (2026-11-04, after a first version that showed only the newest): Revoke all stays in it and asks first, and cards stay fully open rather than collapsing.
    9. The session list's ⋯ menu offers Share too, beside the right-click menu.

    14. Alternatives considered¶

    • A read-only snapshot filtered by the daemon (this spec's first draft): an allowlist of event kinds, tool-call levels, and a secret scan. Safer to hand out, but a guest could not continue the conversation, which the user wants.
    • A token scoped to one session handed to a full web client instead of a guest socket. It would reuse /ws as it is, but every method would then need a scope check, where the guest socket checks one list at its door.
    • Plain GETs and polling for the transcript. Simpler to proxy, but guests need the live stream and the composer's methods, which the client protocol already has.

    15. Implementation notes (2026-11-04)¶

    Implemented in client protocol 1.88.0. Where the code settled what the draft left open, or differs:

    • A link opens a root session. session_share.create refuses a delegated one with shared_session_not_root: its approvals, assets, and tree all hang from the root, so sharing part of a tree had no clear meaning.
    • The check is one guard in the connection loop (ConnectionGuard in the transport, crates/server/src/service/guest.rs). It asks before a request reaches the server and before a queued frame reaches the client, and a test fails until every client method is either in its list or named as refused, and checks each listed method's params really have the member it scopes by.
    • Guests cannot pick an agent or model through a side door. run.start takes a required selection, so it is refused; session.submit_input, session.goal.set, and session.goal.lifecycle are refused when they carry one, or reset_fallback. A guest's page leaves them out, so its runs use the session's selection.
    • Labels live in the origin, not a new event field. Every event already carries its origin, through the queue too, so a guest's is client:guest-<label in base 32>-<8 hex of its tab>, which clients decode (guest_label). History did not change, a label outlives its link, and the link's secret is not written anywhere. It also keeps a label to 30 bytes.
    • Any change to a link closes its guests' connections. A client whose link still works connects again and reads what it may do, and under what label, from the hello. So can_send and the label apply at once, in the page too.
    • A guest reads the model catalog (runtime.snapshot.get, runtime.changed) so the agent and model show by name. That shows provider names and their non-secret setup values, such as a custom endpoint, and never a credential. runtimes.list is refused, so the Runtimes tab shows the session's attachments without their status, and absolute workspace paths. (Since client protocol 1.89.0 a guest reads session.runtimes.list, so it has its session's machines, status, and homes: see The machine on a tool call.)
    • Assets: a guest reads the tree's own assets, and a project's or the shared store's by ID, which it cannot list or guess, rather than only those the transcript shows.
    • The page reads its link's state from its own address. A browser gives no reason for a socket that failed, so when one does the page fetches its own address: 404 shows "This link isn't available", and no answer keeps redialing, as for a daemon that restarts. So the page and the 404 answer any origin.
    • /s/<id>/ws goes through the /s/ route, since the router allows no more specific route beside its wildcard.
    • The web client has no / commands: its compact, goal, and skills are in the ⋮ menu and the session panel. The guest's menu leaves out Rename, Share, Schedule, and Preset.
    • Not done:
    • the top bar names the guest and the expiry, but not who else is connected, which a guest cannot read; the owner sees the count;
    • the link badge is in the title bar, not on sidebar rows;
    • session.delete reports no deleted_shares; its links go with it, and project.delete counts only links of the project's own assets;
    • a guest's message that starts with / still reaches an ACP agent as text, and that agent may treat it as a command. The menu offers a guest only compact and goal, but the daemon cannot tell a command from a message.
